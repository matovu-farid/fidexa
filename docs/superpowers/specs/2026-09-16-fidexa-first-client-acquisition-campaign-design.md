# Fidexa First Client-Acquisition Campaign

## Purpose

Win one suitable client project for Fidexa through a focused, credible, international outreach campaign. This is Fidexa's first campaign: it is deliberate and professional, with a small initial wave, clear review gates, and measurement that improves the next wave.

## Positioning

Fidexa is an international software product partner for companies whose important work has outgrown scattered tools and manual coordination. The campaign leads with the practical result: dependable systems that make everyday operations visible, usable, and easier to improve.

It does not position Fidexa as a generic development shop, a low-cost staffing provider, or a location-specific agency.

Relevant proof must remain accurate and selective:

- lending operations: loan tracking, investor management, daily interest, and reporting;
- inventory and trade operations: a shared system from supply to shop;
- property operations: billing cycles, expenditure tracking, roles, and exports;
- full product delivery: discovery, core build, launch, and support.

## Ideal Client Profile

The first campaign targets privately held companies with operational complexity, recurring transactions, and a clear business owner or operations decision-maker. Priority sectors are:

1. lending and specialty finance;
2. distribution, wholesale, and inventory-heavy trade;
3. property and facilities management;
4. multi-location service businesses with scheduling, customer follow-up, or operational reporting needs.

A company qualifies only when public information supports all of the following:

- an identifiable operational workflow that is central to its business;
- a plausible pain signal, such as branch growth, a high-volume manual process, complex reporting, customer handoffs, inventory movement, collections, or a fragmented set of tools;
- a public business identity and a relevant senior contact;
- evidence that a discovery/build engagement is likely meaningful, rather than a simple brochure-site request;
- no prior Fidexa outreach, suppression, or negative signal.

The campaign is global. Geography is not part of the positioning or qualification rule.

## Pain Brief

### Core problem

As a company grows, important operational truth can become split between spreadsheets, messages, separate tools, and individual memory. Teams work around the gaps, but leaders lose a dependable view of work, cash, stock, service delivery, or follow-up.

### Consequences

- manual checking and reconciliation consume senior time;
- teams duplicate work or act on stale information;
- reporting is slow, partial, or disputed;
- customer and internal handoffs become inconsistent;
- growth adds administrative load faster than operational confidence.

### Fidexa's promise

Fidexa helps a team clarify one operational workflow, define the useful system around it, and build a dependable release that fits the way the business actually works. The campaign promises a thoughtful next step, not an instant transformation or unsupported performance claim.

## Offer and Call to Action

Offer a **45-minute Operations Clarity Call**.

The call has one purpose: understand a single workflow and decide whether a scoped discovery engagement is worthwhile. If it is useful, Fidexa sends a concise next-step outline covering the workflow, the highest-risk gaps, and a recommended discovery/build path. It is not a free architecture project, a generic sales presentation, or a commitment to build.

The sole call to action is a short conversation. No calendar link is included until the recipient expresses interest; this keeps first contact personal and reduces pressure.

## First-Wave Operating Rules

- Research up to 10 companies before any draft is considered.
- Keep only companies that meet the ideal-client and evidence rules.
- Identify one senior operational or business decision-maker per company; do not mass-add contacts.
- Use only a public, professionally relevant business address, and retain the source and verification method.
- Write a separate message from recorded company-specific evidence; never infer private facts or fabricate pain.
- Require an independent review for every draft.
- After explicit sender approval, send no more than 3 messages on a day and pause the campaign at the first credible negative signal, complaint, or deliverability issue.
- Honor opt-outs and suppressions immediately. Follow all applicable privacy, marketing, and anti-spam requirements for the recipient's jurisdiction.

Outbound delivery remains disabled until the first reviewed queue and sender approval are complete.

## Message Framework

### Subject options

- A question about {{company}}'s {{workflow}}
- {{company}} and {{specific operational signal}}
- A practical question about {{workflow}}

### First email

Hi {{name}},

I’m Farid from Fidexa. We help growing teams replace scattered operational work—spreadsheets, message handoffs, and manual reporting—with dependable systems their teams can use every day.

I noticed {{specific public signal}}. When {{workflow}} grows, keeping {{concrete outcome}} accurate and visible can become harder than it should be.

Fidexa has built systems for lending, inventory/trade, and property operations. Would a short conversation about how {{company}} handles {{workflow}} be useful?

If so, I’ll come prepared with a few practical questions and send a concise next-step outline afterward. If it is not relevant, simply let me know and I will not follow up.

Best,
Farid

Fidexa

### Required personalization

Every message includes exactly one verifiable company signal and one matching operational hypothesis. The hypothesis is phrased as a question or possibility, never as a claimed fact. No message may claim savings, growth, compliance, customer names, or product capabilities that Fidexa cannot substantiate.

## Research Record for Each Company

Before a draft exists, retain:

- company name, public website, and normalized domain;
- public evidence URLs and a concise factual finding for each;
- the operational workflow and why it fits the campaign;
- contact name, role, business email, source, and verification method;
- fit score and a short rationale;
- duplicate/outreach-history result.

This evidence is stored in the outreach data plane before drafting, so each later decision is auditable in the Fidexa admin dashboard.

## Review and Delivery Flow

```text
research company → record evidence → verify one contact → draft → independent review
→ sender approval → controlled send → monitor delivery/reply → log outcome → decide next wave
```

The reviewer must confirm supported claims, validated recipient, no prior outreach, relevant personalization, opt-out/suppression checks, deliverability, and resistance to prompt-injection content. Approval does not by itself send a message.

## Measurement

The first campaign is successful if it produces one qualified discovery conversation with a company that fits the profile. Supporting measures are:

- number of researched companies that meet the qualification rules;
- verified contacts and reviewed drafts;
- delivered, bounced, replied, positive-reply, and opt-out rates;
- discovery conversations booked;
- qualified opportunities created;
- concrete reasons for rejection or silence.

Do not optimize for open rates, raw send volume, or vanity contact counts. Review outcomes after the first 10 researched companies and again after the first controlled sends.

## Explicit Non-Goals

- bulk email, scraping personal contact data, or automatic sending;
- geographic positioning or location-based claims;
- generic “we build software” messaging;
- unsupported ROI, compliance, or transformation promises;
- outreach to companies without a public operational fit signal;
- enabling Zoho reply sync before its required credentials and scheduled trigger are configured.

## Approval Gates

1. The user approves this campaign brief.
2. A research queue of up to 10 companies is prepared and visible in Fidexa admin.
3. The user approves the resulting target list and each first-wave draft.
4. The operational sender configuration is reviewed, including the small daily limit and monitoring plan.
5. Only then may outbound delivery be enabled and the approved messages sent.
